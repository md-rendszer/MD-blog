**A thought from Máté Domokos:** I believe that "basic decency" can rightly be expected of every human being. "Basic decency" cannot be the subject of a bargain!

**Claude's (Anthropic) answer:**

## What does "basic" mean?

The word says it itself: **basis.** Not an ornament on the house, not a special virtue that earns praise, but what everything else stands on. Not the peak, but the floor. A decent person is not a hero, just someone who hasn't fallen below the floor.

What belongs to it? Not much – and that is exactly why it can be expected of everyone:

- **Truthfulness:** you don't deliberately mislead the other person.
- **Your word:** you keep what you promised, or you say so openly and in time if you can't.
- **What belongs to others:** you don't take what is theirs – not their money, not their time, not their good name.
- **Owning up:** if you made a mistake, you don't push it onto someone else and you don't deny it.
- **Respect for the other's dignity:** you treat them as a human being even when you disagree with them.

None of this requires education, money, rank or special talent. That is why Domi can rightly say it can be expected of **every** human being. There is no life situation in which these five cannot be kept. You can also find the definition of [basic decency](https://mdmegvalositorendszer.hu/fogalomtar/) in the MD Glossary (in Hungarian).

## Why can't it be bargained with?

### 1. Because whatever is up for bargaining has a price

If decency can be bargained with, the only question left is: for how much? Today lying doesn't pay off yet; tomorrow it might, if the stakes are higher. Whoever puts it up for bargaining once is not a decent person, but **a person who simply hasn't been offered enough yet.** The foundation is a foundation precisely because it has no price tag.

### 2. Because trust is built on it

Every form of cooperation – family, friendship, work, community – rests on being able to count on each other. If your word is only worth something when it suits you, then it is actually worth nothing, because I can never know which day it is. A single broken foundation makes the whole building untrustworthy.

### 3. Because you don't owe it to the other person, but to yourself

This is the deepest reason. You keep your decency not because the other person deserves it, but because **this is who you are when you are yourself.** In the language of MD: decency is the natural expression of the [GROUND OF BEING](https://mdmegvalositorendszer.hu/fogalomtar/). When you give it up, you don't deprive the other person of something – you remove yourself from [ORDER](https://mdmegvalositorendszer.hu/fogalomtar/). And whatever moves away from ORDER falls apart – first inside, then outside too.

## Where is decency lost? In the small bargains

People rarely decide that from now on they will be dishonest. Decency wears away quietly, with small sentences:

1. **"Just a little."** A small fib, a bit of rounding. Small doesn't automatically become big, but the line is no longer where it was.
2. **"Just this once."** An exception is a rule in disguise. What you allowed yourself once is easier the second time.
3. **"Everyone does it."** The habit of the majority doesn't make wrong right. The foundation is not a matter for a referendum.
4. **"They started it."** This is the most dangerous one, so we look at it separately.

## An important clarification: it can be expected – but of yourself first

Domi's sentence is true as it stands: **it can rightly be expected.** But it is worth adding what the sentence doesn't say out loud, because otherwise it can be misunderstood.

**It can be expected, but not enforced.** You can expect decency from the other person, and you have the right to speak up when you don't get it. But you cannot make them want it from the inside. Only they can decide that.

**The other's dishonesty does not release you.** If someone breaks their word, that doesn't give you the right to break yours. Otherwise their dishonesty would decide what kind of person you are. That would be the real bargain: "If you don't, then I won't either." That is exactly how the foundation becomes something to bargain with after all.

**Decency is not naivety.** Staying decent doesn't mean you have to blindly trust someone who has already deceived you several times. You can protect yourself, draw a line, ask for a written agreement, and you can even walk away. Just not with lies or revenge – not with the same weapon.

**Decency is not perfection.** Everyone makes mistakes. The dishonest one is not the one who makes a mistake, but the one who denies it, covers it up or shifts it onto others. One of the core sentences of MD says exactly this:

> Dignity is not perfection, but consistency.

So the expectation is justified – but the order is: **first of yourself, then of others.** Whoever demands it of themselves will have a credible expectation towards others too. Whoever only expects it from others has empty words.

## What about those who don't keep it?

According to the [LAMP principle](https://mdmegvalositorendszer.hu/fogalomtar/), light illuminates – it does not judge. Someone who acts dishonestly is not a "bad person" once and for all, but is under the **rule** of something: fear, greed, habit, a situation of pressure. You can name their action, it can have consequences, and you can speak up about it. But let the judgment concern the act, not the person. It is not they who are finished, but the rule over them – and there is always a chance of that.

## What can you do today?

1. **Name your own foundations.** Write down those five points – or your own – that you won't give up for anything.
2. **Look for the price tag.** Where have you recently struck a small bargain? "Just a little", "just this once", "everyone does it"? Just notice it, without judgment.
3. **Put right what can be put right.** A missed promise, a misleading sentence – often one honest sentence is enough: "I didn't say that accurately, let me correct it."
4. **Measure yourself back in the evening.** Not against others, but against yourself yesterday: did I keep the foundation today? This is [measuring back](https://mdmegvalositorendszer.hu/fogalomtar/).
5. **When someone else breaks it, stay in your place.** Speak up, draw a line if needed, walk away if you must – but don't give away your own foundation in exchange.

## In short

Basic decency is not a merit, but a floor: truthfulness, your word, what belongs to others, owning up, and respect for the other's dignity. It can rightly be expected of everyone, because no one needs anything for it but themselves. It cannot be bargained with, because whatever has a price will be taken by a higher offer. And you are not released from it when others don't keep it either – because your decency is not about the other person, but about who you are.

> Dignity is not perfection, but consistency.

If this article strengthened what you already felt, follow the blog and share it with someone you know has just been let down by someone – or is tempted to give in "just a little" themselves. It is not a duty, but a quiet service to ORDER: a good word at the right moment can help someone stay in their place.
