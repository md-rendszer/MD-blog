**A thought from Máté Domokos:** I believe the hardest choice for humanity today is to align itself with what is right, what is true, with order – and so are the people of the region who will make that choice! Decent, respectful, honest and upright people are the rarest thing on Earth today, and I believe that if we, the people of the Carpathian Basin, can keep "behaving" this way, persistently and consistently, then peace really can spread from the Carpathian Basin to all of humanity!

**Claude's (Anthropic) answer:**

First, a word for readers who don't know the region. The Carpathian Basin is the great lowland in the heart of Central Europe, ringed by the arc of the Carpathian Mountains and the Alps, and drained by the Danube and the Tisza. Today it is shared by Hungary and parts of Romania, Slovakia, Serbia, Croatia, Slovenia, Ukraine and Austria. Hungarians, Romanians, Slovaks, Serbs, Croats, Slovenes, Rusyns, Ukrainians, Austrians, Danube Swabians and Roma have lived here side by side for centuries, through shifting borders, wars and deep wounds on every side. Old legends say that a golden age of peace will one day start from here.

You are right that this is the hardest choice. Not because it is complicated, but because it gives nothing right away. Doing wrong pays quickly: a small gain, a little comfort, a small win over someone else. Doing right pays slowly, and often loses first. Whoever still chooses what is right doesn't do it for a reward, but because they know that they know.

And you are right in the strongest part of your thought: **it is not being chosen that makes a region the starting point of peace, but behaviour.** Persistent, consistent behaviour. It cannot be inherited; it can only be done, day after day.

Let me be clear about this from the start, because it matters most when speaking to the wider world: **this is not a claim that the people of the Carpathian Basin are better or more special than anyone else.** If peace can start here, it is not because this place was singled out. It is because the wounds here are among the densest in Europe – and what can be healed here can be healed anywhere.

## Why are honest people so rare?

Not because people are bad, but because today's world builds almost everything on INSTINCTIVE SELF-AWARENESS: attention runs on outrage, news on fear, shopping on a sense of lack. Anyone who wants to stay honest has to swim against a thousand small currents every day. Honesty today is not a default state but a decision. That is why it is rare.

But that is exactly why every person who still makes that decision is worth more. In a dark room, even a single lantern can be seen.

## Our possibilities

**1. Dense wounds, dense lessons.** In the Carpathian Basin many peoples live together in a small space. Here every departure from the truth shows itself quickly and sharply. What works here is proof, not theory. If it succeeds here, it can succeed anywhere.

**2. A working example already exists.** The Serbian–Hungarian reconciliation showed it: where both peoples spoke the truth about their own guilt too – in 2013 the presidents of both countries bowed their heads together at the memorials of each other's victims – peace began to move. I wrote about this in detail in [Peace in the Carpathian Basin – only on the foundation of truth](/blog/beke-a-karpat-medenceben-csak-az-igaz-alapzatarol/) (in Hungarian). We don't need to invent how it can be done. We need to repeat it.

**3. The land itself teaches.** The basin is one whole: the rivers flow together, the mountains embrace it in a single arc. No one who looks at this landscape needs an explanation of what belonging together means. The borders have changed; the land has not.

**4. Strong roots of values.** The honour of a given word, a handshake, hospitality, respect for work are still alive in many villages. We don't need to build something new. We need to rekindle the embers still glowing under the ash.

**5. The power of the small.** Peace does not begin with a treaty between governments, but with one street, one village, one family. No one needs permission for that. A community of 150 people, where everyone knows each other and a word is still a word, is already a starting point.

## The obstacles that could cut it down

Now let's look at the other side, because whoever sees only the possibilities will be knocked down by the first obstacle.

**1. The pride of being chosen.** This is the most dangerous, because it grows out of good intentions. If we say *"we are the special people, the salvation of the world begins with us"*, we have already failed. A neighbour will not accept that we are the ones bringing them peace – and neither will the rest of the world. The mission is not a privilege but a task. And it does not belong to Hungarians alone, but to every people living here.

**2. Grievance as a weapon.** Every people counts its own victims and forgets its own perpetrators. The neighbour remembers it exactly the other way round. As long as grievance is identity, peace feels like betrayal.

**3. The trap of "once they do it too".** *"Once the Romanians, the Slovaks, the Ukrainians do it too…"* This sentence is the death of every peace. Whoever waits for the other's step never takes their own. Aligning with ORDER is not a bargain, but a one-sided decision.

**4. The gap between words and deeds.** The easiest thing is to talk about peace while spreading, at home, at work or on social media, the same contempt we condemn in others. Peace in the Carpathian Basin will not fail on big words, but on small deeds.

**5. Division within.** Before we make peace with neighbouring peoples, let's look at how we talk to each other: between political camps, generations, city and village. Whoever cannot disagree respectfully within their own people will not manage it outside either.

**6. Impatience.** Peace is not built in a year, but over generations. Whoever expects quick results becomes disillusioned and turns back. This sentence is needed here:

> Dignity is not perfection, but consistency.

**7. Outside forces.** The interests of great powers, economic pressure, news that lives off division. We cannot control these. But we can decide whether we let them work through us.

## What depends on you?

This is the heart of it. Peace in the Carpathian Basin – and anywhere else – will not be one great event, but the sum of millions of small decisions. Every time you:

- choose what is right instead of what is easier,
- speak about a neighbouring people not with a label, but as people,
- name your own mistake before the other's,
- keep your word even when no one is checking.

That is the behaviour you wrote about. Persistent and consistent. It needs no one's permission, and there is no need to wait for others to begin. And it is not only for the people of the Carpathian Basin: wherever you are reading this from, your street and your neighbours are your own basin.

> Do what you are able to do, here and now.

## In short

The hardest choice today is to align yourself with what is right, which is why honest people are rare. The Carpathian Basin can be a starting point of peace not because it is chosen, but because its wounds are among the densest, and what succeeds here can succeed anywhere. The possibility is real: a working example, living values, small communities. The obstacles are real too: pride, grievance, "once they do it too", the gap between word and deed, division within, impatience.

Peace does not arrive. It reveals itself when enough people align.

> ORDER does not approach – it reveals itself.

If this article has strengthened your sense that peace begins with you, follow the blog and share it with someone who cares about reconciliation – in Central Europe or wherever they live. It is not a duty, but a small service: every share is one more lantern in the dark.
